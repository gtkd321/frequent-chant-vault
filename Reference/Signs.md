# Signs

Every sign card in your review deck, in the order you meet them. The plugin includes the Noto Music font, so the glyphs display even without it installed.

| Week | Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | <span class="psq-g">𝁆</span> Ison | Repeat the previous pitch (or, at the start of a piece, sing the base note). |
| 1 | <span class="psq-g">𝁇</span> Oligon | Up one step. |
| 1 | <span class="psq-g">𝁑</span> Apostrophos | Down one step. |
| 1 | <span class="psq-g">𝁉</span> Petasti | Up one step, with its own livelier, flicked delivery. |
| 1 | <span class="psq-g">𝁎</span> Kentimata | Up one step, sung lightly. Usually paired with an oligon. |
| 1 | Yfen (tie) | Joins two characters of the same pitch into one sustained note. Their durations add. |
| 1 | The natural scale of Ni, ascending | Ni – Pa – Vou – Ga – Di – Ke – Zo – Ni' |
| 1 | Diatonic steps from Ni, in moria (1881 Committee) | 12 – 10 – 8 – 12 – 12 – 10 – 8 (72 per octave) |
| 1 | Parallagi vs. metrophonia | Parallagi: singing the note names (Ni, Pa, Vou…) in rhythm. Metrophonia: the note-by-note outline read from the neumes, before the text and full melos are added. |
| 2 | <span class="psq-g">𝁿</span> Klasma | Adds one beat to the note. |
| 2 | <span class="psq-g">𝂅</span> Apli | Adds one beat to the note (written below). |
| 2 | <span class="psq-g">𝂆</span> Dipli | Adds two beats. |
| 2 | <span class="psq-g">𝂇</span> Tripli | Adds three beats. |
| 2 | Rests | A rest takes the place of a note for one or more beats. See [[Ex 028]]–[[Ex 030]]. |
| 3 | <span class="psq-g">𝁓</span> Yporroi | Two quick descending steps, sung as a connected pair. |
| 3 | <span class="psq-g">𝁏</span> Kentima | Two steps up. Always written with an oligon or petasti. |
| 3 | <span class="psq-g">𝁕</span> Elafron | Two steps down. |
| 3 | Leap of a fourth up | Combine an oligon or petasti with a kentima (see [[Ex 045]]). |
| 3 | Leap of a fourth down | Combine an elafron and an apostrophos (see [[Ex 045]]). |
| 4 | <span class="psq-g">𝁐</span> Ypsili | Four steps up. Always written with an oligon or petasti. |
| 4 | <span class="psq-g">𝁖</span> Chamili | Four steps down. |
| 4 | How a fifth up is written | Oligon (or petasti) with an ypsili at its right end. |
| 4 | Elliptic (incomplete) measure | A measure with fewer beats than the meter, usually at the start or end of a phrase. See [[Ex 059]]. |
| 5 | <span class="psq-g">𝂏</span> Gorgon | Speeds things up: the marked note and the one before it share one beat between them. |
| 5 | Continuous elafron | Elafron in running motion, part of a quick descent. See [[Ex 078]]. |
| 6 | <span class="psq-g">𝂒</span> Digorgon | Three notes share one beat. |
| 6 | <span class="psq-g">𝂖</span> Trigorgon | Four notes share one beat. |
| 6 | <span class="psq-g">𝂗</span> Argon | Divides and lengthens at once: the note before is shortened and the marked note held longer. Listen to [[Ex 087]]. |
| 6 | <span class="psq-g">𝂙</span> Diargon | Like the argon, but holds the marked note longer still. See [[Ex 091]]. |
| 6 | <span class="psq-g">𝁘</span> Vareia | Stresses the note that follows it. |
| 6 | <span class="psq-g">𝁚</span> Psifiston | A strong, sharply accented attack on the note. |
| 7 | <span class="psq-g">𝁛</span> Omalon | A smooth, gentle undulation on the note. |
| 7 | <span class="psq-g">𝁜</span> Antikenoma | A light, connected delivery that leans into the next note. Drilled on both weak and strong beats in [[Ex 099]]–[[Ex 101]]. |
| 7 | <span class="psq-g">𝁠</span> Syndesmos / eteron | Binds two notes into one smooth gesture. |
| 7 | <span class="psq-g">𝃏</span> Diesis | Raises the note by a few moria (an accidental sharp). |
| 7 | Yfesis | Lowers the note by a few moria (an accidental flat). |
| 8 | <span class="psq-g">𝂢</span> Martyria | A 'witness' sign: a note letter plus a genus mark that confirms which pitch and scale you're on. |
| 9 | Plagal Fourth: base and genus | Base Ni, diatonic. Intonation: Νεάγιε. |
| 10 | First Mode: base and genus | Base Pa, diatonic. Intonation: Ἀνανές. |
| 11 | Fourth Mode Legetos: base | Vou, diatonic. Agia (papadic) sits on Di. |
| 12 | Plagal First: base | Pa, diatonic (some melodies use low Ke). Intonation: Ἀνεανές. |
| 13 | Third Mode: base and genus | Base Ga, enharmonic (the Nana tetrachord). Intonation: Νανά. |
| 13 | Enharmonic tetrachord from Ga (this vault's model) | Ga–Di 12, Di–Ke 12, Ke–Zo 6 (Zo is lowered). This is an approximation, so check it against your teacher's. |
| 14 | Grave: base | Ga (enharmonic) in the workbook. Some melodies use diatonic low Zo. Intonation: Ἀανές. |
| 15 | Thesis / formula | A stock melodic phrase or cadence for a given mode and genre. Fluent readers recognize these as whole units. |
| 16 | <span class="psq-g">𝃅</span> Hard chromatic tetrachord from Pa | Pa–Vou 6, Vou–Ga 20, Ga–Di 4 |
| 16 | <span class="psq-g">𝃃</span> Soft chromatic tetrachord from Di | Di–Ke 8, Ke–Zo 14, Zo–Ni' 8 |
| 17 | Plagal Second: base and genus | Base Pa, hard chromatic. Intonation: Νεχεανές. |
| 19 | Second Mode: base | Di. Soft chromatic at sticheraric and papadic tempo; heirmologic melodies often take a hard-chromatic coloring. |
| 22 | Heirmologic vs. sticheraric | Heirmologic: quick, roughly one note per syllable (heirmoi, apolytikia). Sticheraric: slower, with more melisma (stichera, doxastika). |
| 38 | <span class="psq-g">𝃂</span> Phthora | A sign that changes the scale or genus from that point on, fixing the identity of a note. |
| 39 | <span class="psq-g">𝃈</span> Chroa | A coloring of the scale (zygos, kliton, spathi) that alters certain steps without fully changing mode. |
| 39 | Attraction (ἕλξις) | A note pulled slightly toward a nearby strong degree, e.g. Zo lowered as it falls to Ke. |
| 40 | <span class="psq-g">𝃇</span> Nenano | A chromatic phthora that gives Plagal Second / tetraphonic coloring, typically around Ga or Di. |
| 43 | Papadic tempo | The slowest, most melismatic style: Cherubic hymns, koinonika, and kalophonic pieces. |
