# Phthorae and Chroai

## Phthorae (φθοραί)

A phthora placed on a note says: *from here, treat this note as degree X of genus Y*. The scale around it changes until another phthora or martyria cancels it.

- **Diatonic phthorae** exist for each degree (Ni, Pa, Vou, Ga, Di, Ke, Zo). They let a melody "re-base", for example making Ke sound like Pa.
- **Hard chromatic phthorae** bring in the Plagal Second scale (Pa–Vou 6, Vou–Ga 20, Ga–Di 4).
- **Soft chromatic phthorae** bring in the Second Mode scale (Di–Ke 8, Ke–Zo 14, Zo–Ni' 8).
- **Enharmonic phthora** brings in the Nana tetrachord of Third Mode.
- **Nenano** is a chromatic phthora used in Plagal Second and tetraphonic passages, typically around Ga or Di.

Reading tip: when you see a phthora, stop for half a second, say the new note name to yourself, and set the drone (below) to the new scale before you sing on.

## Chroai (χρόαι)

Chroai are colorings (zygos, kliton, spathi) that bend a few steps near the note they're written on, without a full change of genus. They mostly appear in sticheraric and papadic pieces. The exact step sizes differ between theory books, so copy your teacher's version into [[Notes]].

## Attractions (ἕλξεις)

Attractions are unwritten adjustments. A note is drawn toward a strong degree nearby, for example Zo lowered as it falls to Ke in First Mode, or Ga raised as it climbs to Di. Workbook [[Ex 103]]–[[Ex 106]] show the written versions (dieseis and yfeseis). In real scores, listen to the recordings to hear the unwritten ones.
